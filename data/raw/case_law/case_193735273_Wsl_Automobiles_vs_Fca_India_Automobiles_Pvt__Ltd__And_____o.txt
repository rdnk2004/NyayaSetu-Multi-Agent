REASONING:
The State Consumer Disputes Redressal Commission's decision to modify its original order rests on the application of its review jurisdiction under Section 50 of the Consumer Protection Act, 2019. The Commission interpreted the statutory threshold of "error apparent on the face of the record" as a clear, obvious, and self-evident mistake in a judicial decision that can be recognized immediately without requiring a lengthy process of reasoning, extensive argument, or the introduction of new evidence. The Commission held that this legal standard permits the correction of an apparent error of law while keeping the underlying factual findings intact, thereby justifying the re-examination of how liability was distributed between the dealer and the manufacturer.

The core substantive reasoning of the Commission is anchored in the statutory framework of product liability under Section 84 of the Consumer Protection Act, 2019. Having previously established through service records and an expert committee report from Punjab Engineering College that the vehicle suffered from "inherent manufacturing defects," the Commission applied the "doctrine of product liability." It reasoned that under Section 84(1)(a) of the Act, a product manufacturer bears the principal liability in any action where the product contains a manufacturing defect. Because the defect was inherent to the manufacturing process, the primary responsibility to refund the purchase consideration and provide consequential relief logically and legally shifts to the manufacturer, rather than the retail dealer.

Furthermore, the Commission relied on established judicial precedents to clarify the distinct liabilities of dealers and manufacturers. Applying the decisions of the Hon'ble Supreme Court in "Hindustan Motors Ltd. and another Versus N. Siva Kumar and another" and the Hon'ble National Commission in "Tata Motors Limited Versus Harpreet Singh," the Commission affirmed the settled legal principle that in cases of manufacturing defects, the manufacturer alone is liable to refund the purchase price and pay compensation, and the dealer cannot be held liable. Consequently, the Commission concluded that fastening the liability of refund and compensation onto the dealer was a clear error of law apparent on the face of the record, requiring the modification of the order to direct all financial liabilities solely to the manufacturer.
