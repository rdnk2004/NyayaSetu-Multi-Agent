DATE: 2008-04-10

REASONING:
The Commission determined that Consumer Fora established under the Consumer Protection Act, 1986, possess jurisdiction to entertain complaints from electricity consumers only when there is an allegation of a specific defect, deficiency in service, or the adoption of an unfair trade practice by the electricity supplier. A consumer aggrieved solely by the actions of an electricity provider initiating assessment proceedings under Section 126 of the Electricity Act, 2003, for the unauthorized use of electricity does not satisfy the statutory definition of a consumer under Section 2(1)(d) of the Consumer Protection Act. This is because such a complainant is not complaining about any inherent deficiency or defect in the service supplied, but is instead disputing a statutory enforcement and recovery action.

Applying the principles of statutory interpretation, the Commission analyzed the relationship between the Electricity Act, 2003—specifically Sections 173, 174, and 175—and Section 3 of the Consumer Protection Act. The court held that there is no inherent conflict or inconsistency between the two legislations, emphasizing that the provisions of both Acts must be harmonized to give them full meaning and effect on the ground. The court applied the legal test of inconsistency, stating that provisions of one Act are inconsistent with another only if they are in direct conflict and cannot be harmonized, meaning effect cannot be given to one without committing a violation or breach of the other. Because Section 126 and Section 127 of the Electricity Act do not offend the scheme of the Consumer Protection Act, and because Section 175 declares the Electricity Act to be in addition to and not in derogation of other laws, the specialized statutory machinery of the Electricity Act must be allowed to operate in full.

Furthermore, the Commission observed that Section 126 is a fiscal provision enacted to curb the vices of theft and unauthorized use of electricity, and such provisions must be construed strictly. The Electricity Act provides a self-contained, validly constituted procedure consisting of an Assessing Officer, an Appellate Authority under Section 127, and Special Courts, reinforced by Section 145 which expressly bars the jurisdiction of Civil Courts. The Commission reasoned that Consumer Fora cannot replace these validly constituted statutory authorities, nor can they sit in appeal over them. Additionally, the Commission highlighted that Section 14 of the Consumer Protection Act does not envisage or empower consumer forums to grant reliefs such as quashing assessment orders. Consequently, allowing consumer forums to assume jurisdiction over matters of assessment, meter tampering, or unauthorized use of electricity would amount to an unauthorized usurpation of jurisdiction and would impermissibly scuttle the designated statutory procedure.
