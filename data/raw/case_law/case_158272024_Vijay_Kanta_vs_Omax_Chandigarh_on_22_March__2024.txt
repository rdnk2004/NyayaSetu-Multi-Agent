CASE_TITLE: Vijay Kanta vs Omax Chandigarh on 22 March, 2024
COURT: State Consumer Disputes Redressal Commission
DATE: 2024-03-22
SOURCE_URL: https://indiankanoon.org/doc/158272024/
ATTRIBUTION: Powered by Indian Kanoon (https://indiankanoon.org)
REASONING:
The State Commission's decision pivots on the statutory interpretation of pecuniary jurisdiction under Section 11 of the Consumer Protection Act, 1986, under which the consumer complaint was originally instituted. Under Section 11(1) of the 1986 Act, the pecuniary jurisdiction of the District Commission was strictly restricted to complaints where the aggregate "value of the goods or services and the compensation, if any, claimed" did not exceed twenty lakh rupees. The Commission applied this statutory test to the factual matrix of the case, noting that the basic cost of the unit was fixed at Rs. 94,74,461.15 and the actual total consideration paid by the complainants to the builder stood at Rs. 1,02,52,655.34. When aggregating the value of the unit paid by the complainants with the specific reliefs claimed—namely, Rs. 5 lakhs as compensation for mental agony and interest at 18% per annum on delayed possession—the total valuation exceeded Rs. 1.10 crores.

The State Commission rejected the District Commission’s reasoning that the procedural transition to the Consumer Protection Act, 2019 allowed the lower forum to retain the matter. The Commission held that under the 1986 Act, any claim exceeding one crore rupees fell exclusively within the pecuniary jurisdiction of the Hon’ble National Commission. Furthermore, the Commission observed that even if the dispute were evaluated under Section 34 of the newer Consumer Protection Act, 2019, which limits the District Commission's jurisdiction to where the value of the goods or services paid as consideration does not exceed Rs. 1 crore, the complaint would still fail the jurisdictional threshold. Since the undisputed total consideration paid by the complainants was Rs. 1,02,52,655.34, it exceeded the statutory limit of Rs. 1 crore under the new Act as well.

Consequently, the State Commission determined that because the District Commission completely lacked the pecuniary jurisdiction to entertain and decide the consumer complaint from its inception, the impugned order was a nullity in the eyes of law. A forum cannot adjudicate a dispute where the monetary value of the transaction and the compensation claimed exceeds its express statutory boundaries. The Commission therefore set aside the District Commission's order and dismissed the consumer complaint solely on the ground of want of pecuniary jurisdiction, while granting the complainants liberty to file a fresh complaint before the appropriate legal platform holding the requisite pecuniary and territorial jurisdiction.
