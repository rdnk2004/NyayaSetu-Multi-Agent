CASE_TITLE: M/S Four Wings Travel & Tours,Through ... vs Shri Satyajit Sagoisem on 17 April, 2017
COURT: State Consumer Disputes Redressal Commission
DATE: 2017-04-17

REASONING:
The Commission addressed the issue of territorial jurisdiction by interpreting the expression "cause of action" under the light of established Supreme Court precedents, including Kandimalla Raghvaiah and Company v. National Insurance Company and Gurdit Singh v. Munsha Singh. The court observed that "cause of action" is a term of wide import, representing a "bundle of facts" that forms the foundation of the suit and entitles the plaintiff to the relief prayed for. Since the consignment of electronic goods was booked in New Delhi with the specific contractual obligation to deliver them to the complainant's residence in Imphal, the subsequent failure to deliver one of the two packets occurred at the destination point. Therefore, the court held that a material part of the cause of action arose at Imphal, which legally entitled the complainant as dominus litis to attract the jurisdiction of the District Consumer Disputes Redressal Forum, Imphal, as his forum conveniens.

Regarding the quantum of compensation for the lost cargo, the Commission rejected the appellant's contention that there was no basis for valuing the lost 24 kg packet at Rs. 15,000. Although the terms of the "Airway Bill" contract stipulated a default liability of Rs. 450 per kilogram for undeclared values, the Commission applied the legal test articulated by the Supreme Court in State of Haryana & Anr. v. Jasbir Kaur & Ors. The court reasoned that the measure of damages cannot be arrived at by precise mathematical calculations alone, and any method adopted for assessing compensation must be weighed against the pivotal standard of "just" compensation. The expression "just" denotes equitability, fairness, reasonableness, and a non-arbitrary, judicious approach. Under this standard, the court held that the District Forum's assessment of Rs. 15,000 was neither a windfall nor a pittance, but rather a rational, fair, and reasonable valuation of the lost packet that did not warrant appellate interference.

Finally, the Commission justified its decision to dismiss the appeal by defining the scope of appellate review, citing Alijan Nanhe Pehalwan Qureshi v. State of Maharashtra and Ramakant Rai v. Madan Rai. The court reasoned that when a First Appellate Court concurs with the findings and conclusions of the trial forum on the evidence, it is not legally obligated to restate the effect of the evidence or elaborately reiterate the reasons already recorded. It is sufficient for the appellate authority to indicate through a reasoned judgment that it has actively and independently applied its mind to the material questions of fact and law. Finding no merit in the grounds of appeal and concurring with the lower forum's determination of liability and damages, the Commission affirmed the trial court's order.
