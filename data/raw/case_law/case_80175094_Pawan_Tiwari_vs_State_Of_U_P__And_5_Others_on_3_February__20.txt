CASE_TITLE: Pawan Tiwari vs State Of U.P. And 5 Others on 3 February, 2021
COURT: Allahabad High Court
DATE: 2021-02-03

REASONING:
The court emphasized that the speedy implementation of the Consumer Protection Act, 2019 is an absolute imperative, rooted in the drastic transformation of consumer markets since the predecessor Act of 1986. By analyzing the Statement of Objects and Reasons of the 2019 Act, the court observed that the emergence of global supply chains, rise in international trade, and rapid development of e-commerce have drastically altered market dynamics. While these advancements provide new options, they have simultaneously rendered the consumer vulnerable to new forms of unfair trade, unethical business practices, and misleading advertisements. The court reasoned that addressing these myriad and constantly emerging vulnerabilities requires appropriate and swift executive interventions to prevent consumer detriment.

Furthermore, the court highlighted that the Central Government had already laid down the necessary legal infrastructure by framing comprehensive rules and regulations under Sections 101, 103, and 104 of the Act of 2019. These include statutory provisions for e-commerce, mediation, electronic filing, and administrative control. The court pointed out that under Section 102 of the Act, the Central Government had also established the Model Rules of 2020, which fully empower the State Government to take immediate steps regarding the salaries, allowances, and service conditions of the members of the State and District Commissions during the transitional period. 

Consequently, the court determined that the State Government must act with corresponding urgency to operationalize the consumer protection machinery. The court ruled that administrative processes, such as obtaining inter-departmental clearances for state-specific rules, should not result in prolonged delays that jeopardize consumer interests. Therefore, leveraging the statutory frameworks and Model Rules already in place, the court held that the State must expeditiously establish and constitute the various District Consumer Protection Councils, Consumer Mediation Cells, and panels of mediators to ensure the effective and speedy disposal of consumer disputes as intended by the legislature.
